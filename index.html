03 - Aim & Hypothesis

What we set out to test

Aim

Build a device that measures water purity by shining a laser through a water sample and detecting how much light passes through.

Hypothesis

Cleaner water lets more light through, so the LDR's resistance will be low for pure water and high for muddy or dirty water.

04 - The Core Concept

Turbidity: a measure of cloudiness

Turbidity is the cloudiness of water caused by suspended particles - mud, silt, and microbes.

More particles scatter and absorb more light, so less light passes straight through - that drop is exactly what the dial to the right tracks as you scroll.

Measured in
NTU
Nephelometric Turbidity Units
0.0 NTU · clean sample
WHO Guideline
Below 5 NTU, ideally under 1 NTU

06 - The Sensor

LDR: light dependent resistor

An LDR is a sensor whose electrical resistance changes with the amount of light hitting it.

Its resistance is a direct proxy for how much light reached it - exactly what we need to measure.

Bright Light

Low Resistance
~ a few hundred Ω

Darkness

High Resistance
~ megohms (MΩ)

09 - Real-Life Applications

Where turbidity testing is used

Water treatment plants check clarity before & after filtration

Environmental agencies monitor rivers, lakes & groundwater

Pools & aquariums track water clarity for safety

Food & beverage checks clarity of juice, beer & milk

Wastewater plants confirm treatment before discharge

10 - Conclusion & Future Scope

What we learned, and what's next

Summary

A cheap, quick, and reasonably accurate way to compare water purity using light.

Limitations

Gives relative, not exact, NTU readings - affected by water colour and stray light.

Future Scope

Calibrate against a real turbidity meter, and add an Arduino with a digital display for automatic readings.
